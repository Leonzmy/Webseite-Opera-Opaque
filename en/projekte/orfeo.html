Orfeo and Euridice have spent a long life together and grown old side by side. Now Euridice has died. Orfeo finds himself alone in his apartment, withdrawing from the world around him. Only his adult son Apollo tries to stay in touch with the increasingly unreachable man.

An old melody begins to haunt Orfeo's mind, carrying him back to his youth. Long-dead friends appear, celebrating the wedding of Euridice and Orfeo. But the veil of ageing settles over everything, and the celebration turns into a funeral procession. Orfeo's apartment transforms into the underworld, becoming his own private hell. The cramped rooms widen into an endless landscape, in which the frail old man finds himself helpless. The river Styx begins to flood the apartment. On its bank sits a lost diver, whom the ship's crew presumably believed dead. Searching for a way out, Orfeo sings into the emptiness, but only the echo answers. Will a boat come to carry him across the water?

Orfeo as the eternal myth of ageing — within the 60 square metres of the neighbouring apartment, and within the inner infinity of solitude.
